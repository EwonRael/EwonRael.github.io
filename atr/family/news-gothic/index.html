About the font

News Gothic was first drawn by Morris Fuller Benton for the American Type Founders, and was released in 1908. As the name implies, it was made to be used in newspapers, and its simple, neutral, and practical construction reflects this. It is a legible and practical choice for body text and headlines alike.

Morris Fuller Benton also drew the bolder Franklin Gothic, which was released around the same time. Although these fonts differ in construction in meaningful ways, it is clear they are of the same sort and today are generally considered part of the same font family. Franklin Gothic was named after the American founding father (who was known to be a bit of a type nerd himself), and although this font is neutral and practical, it does have a subtle American flair to it. Over its century+ existence the font family has embedded itself into American culture. It is the sans-serif typeface of choice used by the New York Times, and is featured in the iconic Star Wars opening exposition text crawl.


News Gothic page in 1923 American Type Founders Specimen Book & Catalogue

Over the years, some of the idiosyncrasies and inconsistencies have been rounded out, and digital revivals often adjust letter construction to appeal to modern sensibilities. The ATR revival faithfully preserves the unique qualities of the original 1908 release. The font features a robust set of special characters and symbols. It supports over 300 languages.
