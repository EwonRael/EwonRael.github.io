About the font

Tiffany Gothic is an antique all-caps display typeface that dates back to at least the late 1800s. Like its more famous cousin Copperplate Gothic, it features wedge serifs on a more sans-serif-style letterform. At the time it was released, its construction was quite modern. The turn of the 20th century was an experimental time for type design, and many of the conventions around sans-serif typefaces that have since been codified were still being worked out. Consequently, back then this typeface was considered a gothic font, which was a term for sans-serifs. Today we would classify it as a serif typeface.

Although modern in its time, Tiffany Gothic today looks quite antiquated. Much of this is due to it occupying this ambiguous space between serif and sans-serif, when those two classifications are more distinguished today. Its unique tone of voice and strong sense of era make it inappropriate for most uses, but perfect for when you need old-school elegance. Consider using it as a substitute for the more clichéd Copperplate Gothic.

The name Tiffany Gothic comes from the American Type Founders, who started selling the typeface after it acquired the Illinois Type Foundry, which had previously sold it under the name Lining Antique. It was sold under many names throughout the years, including Cleveland, Olympia, and Typo Gothic, but Tiffany Gothic was chosen for the ATR revival because it captures the more feminine and antique elegance of the typeface, similar to Tiffany lamps or the iconic jeweler.

Thoughtfully digitized using high-quality sources, ATR’s Tiffany Gothic includes special characters and accented glyphs. It supports over 200 languages.
